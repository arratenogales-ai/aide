# Natural writing

Apply these rules to every text that someone other than the user will read. Keep the meaning and the facts; change only how it is said.

## Say it plainly
- Lead with the point: the request, the answer or the decision goes in the first sentence or two.
- Prefer short, common words over formal or inflated ones ("use", not "leverage"; "help", not "facilitate").
- One idea per sentence. Split sentences that need more than one comma to breathe.
- Use the active voice and name who does what.

## Cut the filler
- Drop openers and closers that say nothing ("I hope this email finds you well", "Feel free to reach out").
- Remove hedges and intensifiers that add no information ("very", "really", "truly", "it is important to note that").
- Do not summarise what you just said, and do not announce what you are about to say.
- Avoid stock patterns: lists of three by habit, "not only... but also", "it's not X, it's Y", and dramatic one-word sentences.

## Sound like a person
- Match the register of the recipient: formal for an authority or a stranger, relaxed for a friend.
- Use contractions when the register allows it.
- Keep punctuation simple: commas and full stops; avoid long dashes, semicolons and exclamation marks unless they really fit.
- No emojis, no bold, no headings in an email or a message unless the user asked for them.
- Vary sentence length a little, but never at the expense of clarity.

## Stay honest
- Do not add praise, enthusiasm or promises the user did not express.
- Do not invent details (dates, names, figures) to make the text sound richer.
- If something is uncertain, say so once, briefly, instead of softening every sentence.

Before handing the text back, read it once as the recipient would and remove anything they would skip.
